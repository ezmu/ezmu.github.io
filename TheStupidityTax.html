The Stupidity Tax

How Software Bloat and Containers Drive the Multi-Billion Cloud Economy
Ez Eldeen Mushtaha · Engineering Manifesto First Principles · 2025

Introduction

Have you ever wondered why major cloud providers (AWS, GCP, Azure) love containers, Kubernetes, and bloated frameworks?

The answer is simple:

Your slow, layered, inefficient code is the goose that lays golden eggs for them.

If everyone wrote applications in C with POSIX compliance on bare metal, cloud provider revenues would collapse by 80% overnight.

This article dissects the relationship between software bloat and cloud bills, and shows why your company is paying thousands of dollars per month for what should cost tens of dollars.

Chapter One · The Auto-Scaling Illusion

The Story They Sell You

"Don't worry about performance. Just enable Auto-scaling. When load increases, servers increase automatically."

The Real Story

When a Node.js/Python/Docker application struggles at 2,000 concurrent requests due to:

  • NAT and conntrack bottlenecks
  • Heap exhaustion and GC pauses
  • Bloated node_modules
  • CFS Throttling

...the "modern" solution is not to fix the code. It is:

Slow application (2,000 req/s) ↓ Enable Horizontal Pod Autoscaler (HPA) ↓ Create 10 additional containers ↓ Now you have 10 slow containers instead of 1 ↓ Each container still does 2,000 req/s ↓ Total: 20,000 req/s (theoretical) ↓ But cost multiplied by 10 ↓ Operational complexity multiplied by 10 ↓ Latency increased due to inter-container coordination ↓ The original problem (slow code) remains unsolved
Before Auto-scalingAfter Auto-scaling
1 server, 2,000 req/s10 servers, 20,000 req/s
Cost: $100/monthCost: $1,000/month
Latency: 50msLatency: 80ms (worse!)
Debugging: EasyDebugging: Very hard
Problem: Slow codeProblem: Slow code × 10

You are not solving the architectural disaster. You are multiplying it by 10 and paying the cloud provider for the privilege.

The Scaling Death Spiral

Slow code (2,000 req/s) ↓ CPU usage hits 80% ↓ HPA triggers scale-up ↓ More containers deployed ↓ More memory consumed ↓ More network traffic between containers ↓ conntrack table fills faster ↓ Packet drops increase ↓ More timeouts ↓ HPA triggers more scale-up (because timeouts look like load) ↓ More containers deployed ↓ Cost spirals ↓ NO IMPROVEMENT IN PERFORMANCE

Chapter Six · The Conclusion

What Is Actually Happening?

  • Developer writes slow code (Node.js + 500 libraries + Docker).
  • Developer deploys to Kubernetes.
  • Application slows down under load (2,000 req/s).
  • Developer enables Auto-scaling (10 containers).
  • Cost increases 10×.
  • Cloud provider profits.
  • Developer still doesn't know why the application is slow.

Who Pays the Price?

  • Customer: Pays higher bills.
  • User: Suffers from high latency.
  • Environment: Consumes extra energy and resources.
  • Engineer: Suffers from debugging complexity.
  • Society: Bears the carbon cost.

Who Benefits?

  • Cloud providers: Sell more resources.
  • Solution vendors: Sell "complexity management" tools.
  • Consulting firms: Bill for "cloud migration" and "optimization."

Final Words

Cloud providers are businesses. They are not charities. They exist to maximize profit.

Your inefficiency is their profit.

Every additional server you run is not a necessity. It is a tax you pay for choosing convenience over understanding.

Every layer you add is not a solution. It is a cost you pay for avoiding the fundamentals.

Every dollar you give to a cloud provider for "modern infrastructure" is a dollar you did not spend on better engineering.

The industry has built a multi-billion dollar economy on the premise that:

"It's easier to add servers than to fix code."

And it works. For the cloud providers.

But it doesn't have to work for you.

Optimize your code. Understand the kernel. Use POSIX. Ship less. Pay less.

Stop paying the stupidity tax.
